# Category Authority: Cookware / Kitchen Equipment

## Purpose
Own behavior specific to cookware and durable kitchen equipment when the `Cookware / Kitchen Equipment` Category is active.

Use the Cookware / Kitchen Equipment sections of `criteria.md`, `source_playbooks.md`, and `pricing.md` alongside this file. Inherit Home & Kitchen behavior.

# Shared domain behavior

## Construction drives cooking behavior
Translate materials and construction into heat response, heat distribution, retention, weight, warping resistance, surface behavior, handle temperature, oven limits, and maintenance. Do not rank cookware from layer count, metal name, or thickness alone without the use consequence.

## Surface systems have different lifecycles
Stainless, seasoned iron/steel, enamel, ceramic-style coatings, and fluoropolymer nonstick solve different problems. Separate initial cooking behavior from long-term wear, maintenance, repairability, and expected replacement cycle.

## Compatibility is part of the product
Stovetop type, burner size, induction behavior, oven/broiler limits, dishwasher guidance, utensil compatibility, lid/handle constraints, and storage can change whether otherwise good equipment fits the user's kitchen.

## Cooking tests must match the claim
Use relevant tests for heat distribution, responsiveness, searing, sticking/release, pouring, handling, durability, and cleanup. One highly specific cooking task does not establish universal performance.

# Product Research contributions

## Merge: Product Research -> Scope and decision model
When material, add:
- cooking tasks and batch size;
- stovetop/oven/broiler constraints;
- desired heat response/retention behavior;
- weight/handle/ergonomic limits;
- tolerance for seasoning, hand washing, polishing, or coating replacement;
- dishwasher/storage constraints.

## Merge: Product Research -> Candidate extraction
Add where material:
- construction/cladding or body material relevant to behavior;
- cooking-surface system;
- handle attachment/ergonomics;
- oven and cooktop compatibility;
- maintenance posture;
- likely lifecycle/replaceability of wear surfaces.

## Merge: Product Research -> Hard gates and identity
Add checks for:
- cooktop compatibility and usable base size;
- required oven/broiler temperature limits;
- unacceptable maintenance/coating constraints;
- weight/handle limitations when explicit;
- exact line/construction when similarly named variants differ materially.

## Merge: Product Research -> Comparison and evidence use
Prefer cooking tests whose task maps to the claimed advantage. Connect construction to observed behavior rather than repeating marketing terminology. Treat authoritative food-contact/material claims separately from performance testing.

# Pricing Tiers contributions

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Tier validity
A higher cookware/equipment tier should be justified by meaningful construction, thermal behavior, durability, ergonomics, QC, serviceability, or craft/finish. Layer count, exotic material labels, celebrity branding, or decorative finish alone do not establish proportional functional value.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Marginal-spend analysis
State whether extra spend buys better thermal execution, lower warping risk, stronger handles/hardware, longer surface life, easier service/replacement, better ergonomics, broader compatibility, or primarily finish/craft/brand value.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Diminishing-returns interpretation
Separate functional cooking gains from finish, craft, prestige, or collection value once basic construction and task fit are already strong.

# Vendor Research contributions

## Merge: Vendor Research -> Vendor normalization
For cookware/kitchen-equipment brands add where material:
- construction consistency across lines;
- coating/surface lifecycle reputation;
- handle/hardware and replacement-part support;
- warranty exclusions that matter in ordinary use;
- continuity of lids, seals, handles, or other replaceable components;
- repair/refinishing/reseasoning ecosystem where applicable.

# Quick Check contributions

## Merge: Quick Check -> Target identity
Verify exact size, line, construction, surface/coating generation, handle/lid variant, and regional model when those change compatibility or performance.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Hard gates
Check cooktop/oven compatibility, usable dimensions, maintenance requirements, and explicit material/surface constraints before judging value.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Decision questions
Useful narrow questions include whether the construction actually produces the claimed cooking benefit, whether the wear surface has an acceptable expected life, and whether the product's maintenance burden matches the user.
