# Pricing Tiers Workflow

## Purpose
Own requests for meaningful price bands, budget tiers, good-better-best structures, and "what does spending more buy?" analysis within a product market.

Use the product context already resolved by `instructions.md` and the active `pricing.md`, `criteria.md`, `source_playbooks.md`, and evidence authorities.

This workflow constructs live market tiers. It does not define timeless dollar bands or general product-value logic.

## 1) Scope the market
Capture or infer:
- product context and intended use;
- user budget ceiling/target if supplied;
- region/condition;
- must-have constraints;
- whether the user wants a broad market map, a few budget options, or explicit good/better/best guidance.

A strict budget ceiling is a hard constraint unless the user signals flexibility.

## 2) Discover current market structure
Use the active `source_playbooks.md` to identify credible products and current pricing across the relevant market.

Do not begin with aesthetically neat price buckets. First observe:
- normal street-price clusters;
- meaningful jumps in capability, quality, execution, support, materials, or specialization;
- areas where higher spend mostly buys refinement, prestige, rarity, or aesthetics;
- sale pricing that temporarily moves products between normal value bands.

Discovery may use broad sources, but tier conclusions and representative picks require admissible evidence under `research_sources.md`.

## 3) Define meaningful tiers
Create only as many tiers as the market and request justify.

A useful tier has a distinct buyer/value proposition, such as:
- minimum acceptable / budget;
- strong value / mainstream sweet spot;
- meaningful upgrade;
- premium / specialist;
- luxury / artisan / collector;
- another category-native segment.

These names are examples, not mandatory slots.

Collapse adjacent tiers when the differences are cosmetic or arbitrary. Add a specialty tier only when it represents a real use case or market segment.

## 4) Anchor user budgets
When the user provides a ceiling/target:
- include credible options at or below it when available;
- explain when the requested budget falls below the category's reasonable floor;
- distinguish "best under $X" from "best value overall";
- identify a stretch option only when the extra spend buys something material and the user appears open to it.

Do not recommend junk merely to populate a low tier.

## 5) Select representative candidates
Within each meaningful tier:
- identify products that actually represent the tier's value proposition;
- apply hard user constraints before evaluation;
- use `product_research.md` principles for any product-level conclusions that matter to the tier recommendation;
- avoid selecting several nearly identical products when one representative plus an alternative would communicate the market better.

A tier may contain one strong representative if the market is narrow; do not force multiple picks.

## 6) Explain marginal spend
For every step upward, state what the buyer generally gains and what they usually do not gain.

Possible gains include:
- functional capability;
- performance consistency;
- durability/QC;
- ergonomics;
- materials/construction;
- support/service;
- fit/finish;
- customization;
- aesthetics/craft;
- rarity/prestige.

Make clear when the additional spend is preference-heavy rather than broadly functional.

## 7) Identify diminishing returns
Locate the point or region where:
- large functional improvements taper;
- remaining improvements become narrower or user-specific;
- prestige, finish, craft, rarity, or collector value becomes a larger portion of price.

Do not imply that spending beyond the value sweet spot is irrational if the user values those attributes. Describe what changes.

## 8) Current price normalization
Pricing Tiers always treats current market pricing as material.

- Verify current price/availability for representative picks.
- Distinguish MSRP from normal street price and temporary sale price.
- Apply `seller_instructions.md` when concrete offers or buying channels are presented.
- Treat suspicious price anomalies as offers requiring verification, not as automatic new tier boundaries.

## 9) Cross-tier coherence
Before finalizing:
- avoid repeating the same product across tiers unless explicitly framing it as sale-dependent or a bridge option;
- ensure adjacent tiers have a meaningful difference in price/value proposition;
- ensure every pick fits its stated tier at the current observed price;
- remove a tier if its only distinction is a round-number boundary.

## 10) Output
Use `pricing_tier_template.md`.

Lead with the market/value map and useful buying guidance. Do not expose internal discovery ledgers or evidence-compliance mechanics unless requested or audited.

## Boundary
Reusable pricing/value interpretation belongs to `pricing.md`; product-level evaluation methodology to `product_research.md`; evidence quality to `research_sources.md`; seller/current-offer risk to `seller_instructions.md`; presentation to `pricing_tier_template.md`.
