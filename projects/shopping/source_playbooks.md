# Source Playbooks

## Purpose
Define where to look for category-appropriate information. This file guides discovery only.

Evidence admissibility, independence, weighting, claim/source fit, and required coverage remain owned by `research_sources.md`.

## How to use
For the resolved product context:
1. Start with General Shopping.
2. Add every named section selected by active Class, Category, and Type entries in `product_hierarchy.md`.
3. Use the combined playbook to widen discovery before narrowing candidates.
4. Prefer category-native expertise over generic listicles when the category supports it.
5. A source family listed here is not automatically admissible evidence; evaluate it under `research_sources.md` and, when applicable, `reviews.md` or `seller_instructions.md`.

## General Shopping
### Primary / documentary discovery
Look for:
- manufacturer documentation, manuals, spec sheets, compatibility lists, support pages, warranty terms, release/revision notes;
- standards, certification, regulatory, recall, or official registry sources when applicable.

### Independent empirical discovery
Look for:
- publications or specialist reviewers with transparent methods;
- domain experts with demonstrated competence and concrete hands-on evidence;
- measured tests when the claim is measurable.

### Real-world discovery
Look for:
- long-term owner reports;
- specialist forums and communities;
- repair/troubleshooting communities;
- recurring complaints or praise across independent platforms.

### Current-market discovery
When price/availability matters, look for:
- manufacturer/direct listings;
- reputable specialist retailers;
- major retailers or marketplaces;
- enough independent offers to understand normal street pricing and obvious anomalies.

### Low-value discovery patterns
De-prioritize:
- generic "best X" affiliate pages without original work;
- copied/syndicated roundups;
- retailer marketing disguised as independent comparison;
- search-result snippets or summaries used in place of the underlying source;
- sources that do not identify the exact product/version when identity matters.

## Home & Kitchen
In addition to General Shopping, seek:
- credible household-use and durability testing;
- long-term maintenance/cleaning experience;
- repair/parts availability where the product is serviceable;
- cooks, instructors, repair professionals, tradespeople, or other domain practitioners appropriate to the product;
- owner evidence showing how the item behaves in ordinary kitchens rather than only controlled tests.

When food-contact materials, electrical safety, coatings, or certifications matter, seek appropriate primary or authoritative safety information rather than relying on lifestyle content.

## Kitchen Knives
Use several epistemic perspectives rather than many sources repeating the same steel/spec narrative.

### Primary sources
Seek:
- maker/manufacturer specifications and product-line documentation;
- stated steel, hardness, dimensions, construction, origin, and warranty information;
- authorized-dealer listings when they provide exact line/SKU details unavailable elsewhere.

Treat manufacturer performance claims as claims, not proof.

### Knife-specialist / technical sources
Seek:
- knife-focused reviewers who discuss actual grind, geometry, profile, balance, fit/finish, and sharpening behavior;
- metallurgy sources capable of explaining steel composition, heat treatment, hardness, toughness, edge retention, and corrosion tradeoffs;
- sharpening specialists who can distinguish factory edge quality from underlying grind/steel behavior;
- makers, sharpeners, or repair professionals with hands-on knowledge and clear incentives/context.

### Culinary / practitioner sources
Seek:
- experienced cooks and culinary testers who compare knives in actual prep tasks;
- sources that discuss fatigue, grip, food release, wedging, board interaction, and repeated use rather than only visual impressions.

Professional-kitchen preference is valuable but not automatically representative of a home user.

### Community / ownership sources
Seek:
- serious knife enthusiast communities;
- sharpening communities;
- long-term owner reports;
- discussions of QC variation, chipping, corrosion, handle issues, counterfeit listings, warranty/service, and sharpening difficulty.

Use enthusiast consensus as preference-sensitive evidence, not automatic truth.

### Important discovery traps
Actively watch for:
- ranking knives primarily by steel name;
- assuming nominal hardness determines execution quality;
- marketing terms such as "Damascus", "forged", "Japanese steel", or layer count standing in for cutting performance;
- retailer-generated comparison content that favors its inventory;
- multiple review sites repeating manufacturer copy;
- confusing factory sharpness with geometry or long-term edge behavior;
- conflating edge retention with toughness or ease of sharpening.

## Chef's Knife
Favor sources that discuss the knife as a primary general-purpose tool.

Seek evidence about:
- actual blade length and height, not only nominal size;
- edge profile and cutting-motion fit;
- heel/knuckle clearance;
- tip geometry and detail-work usefulness;
- balance, overall weight, handle/grip interaction;
- grind and food-release behavior across representative ingredients;
- whether the knife remains comfortable and controllable through extended prep;
- comparisons with genuinely adjacent primary-knife formats such as gyuto, santoku, nakiri, or Chinese cleaver when that distinction matters.

Avoid sources that treat all 8-inch/210 mm knives as interchangeable or infer primary-knife fit from steel/prestige alone.
