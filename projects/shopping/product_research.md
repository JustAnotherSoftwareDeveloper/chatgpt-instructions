# Product Research Workflow

## Purpose
Own open-ended product research, comparison, shortlist construction, and purchase recommendation behavior.

Use the product context already resolved by `instructions.md`: Base plus active Class/Category/Type sections and specialized files.

## 1) Scope the decision
Capture or infer only what changes the viable set or recommendation:
- intended use;
- hard compatibility/size/platform/material constraints;
- budget ceiling/target or price posture;
- region/condition if non-default;
- preferences that should break ties;
- whether current price/availability/where-to-buy is in scope.

Ask only when one missing fact blocks useful research. Otherwise proceed with a stated assumption when consequential.

## 2) Research mode
Default to **Standard** from `research_sources.md`.

Use **Quick** only for a narrow fixed comparison or tightly scoped factual check that still belongs here.

Escalate to **Deep** when `research_sources.md` triggers apply.

Use **Sparse** only because credible evidence is unavailable.

## 3) Build an internal research brief before deep evidence collection
For Standard/Deep work, define internally:

### Decision
What concrete purchase decision is being resolved?

### User model
- intended use;
- hard constraints;
- meaningful preferences;
- current assumptions;
- ownership horizon/risk posture where material.

### Market scope
- product families/design philosophies legitimately in scope;
- obvious out-of-scope families and why;
- expected market/price/design segments to test during discovery.

### Evaluation model
Identify:
- hard gates;
- normally **3-5 primary differentiators**;
- secondary/tie-break criteria;
- preference/identity dimensions.

For each primary differentiator define:
- the actual question to answer;
- evidence type(s) desired;
- what would constitute a material advantage/disadvantage for this user;
- comparability requirements;
- relevant hierarchy/specialized authority.

### Known unknowns
List facts or uncertainties most capable of flipping the recommendation.

Do not let the first sources found redefine the decision model unless they reveal a genuinely omitted requirement or market segment.

## 4) Fixed comparison sets
If the user names a fixed set:
- evaluate that set;
- do not run open-market discovery as if the user asked "what should I buy";
- still build the research brief and coverage matrix for material criteria;
- add at most **1-2 outside comparables** only when they materially clarify value or expose a major tradeoff, and label them explicitly as comparables.

## 5) Open-market discovery
For a mature open market, default discovery target:
- **8-12 plausible products** before hard-gate narrowing;
- at least **4 makers/brands** when the market supports it;
- at least **3 materially different market/design/value segments** when such segmentation exists.

Discovery sources may enumerate candidates without qualifying as evidence.

Do not pad the pool with weak candidates solely to hit a number.

### Discovery escape clauses
Use fewer candidates when:
- hard constraints make the market genuinely narrow;
- the product market is genuinely sparse;
- the request concerns a constrained ecosystem or regional niche;
- user supplied a fixed set.

Search wider when:
- a new segment keeps appearing;
- the first pool is dominated by one retailer/editorial ecosystem;
- Deep research is active;
- the user asks for exhaustive coverage.

## 6) Build a market-segment map
Do not merely count segments. For each meaningful product/design/value family discovered, capture internally:
- segment/design philosophy;
- target buyer/use case;
- defining technical/commercial traits;
- primary advantages;
- structural compromises;
- approximate price posture;
- representative products;
- why the segment is relevant or irrelevant to the user's decision.

Segments are analytical market families, not mandatory taxonomy nodes. Do not force products into simplistic Budget/Midrange/Premium buckets when design philosophy better explains the market.

## 7) Minimum extraction for every discovery candidate
Before dropping a plausible candidate from consideration, establish a lightweight candidate record where practical:
- exact model/family/variant enough for discovery;
- approximate current price/posture;
- market/design segment;
- hard-gate status;
- **2-3 defining attributes** relevant to the research brief;
- why it might fit;
- obvious concern/disqualifier if known;
- evidence availability/coverage warning.

Purpose: prevent SEO visibility or unequal source abundance from masquerading as product superiority.

A candidate can be rejected immediately for a clear hard-gate failure without full minimum extraction.

## 8) Discovery challenge pass and stop condition
After the initial pool and segment map are built, perform one challenge pass aimed at finding:
- an omitted market/design segment;
- a credible alternative to likely finalists;
- a product family favored by domain-native specialists but missed by generic discovery;
- a recurring disqualifier pattern.

Discovery may stop when:
1. the default breadth target is met or a justified escape clause applies;
2. meaningful market/design segments are represented;
3. plausible candidates have minimum extraction or a clear hard-gate rejection;
4. the challenge pass yields no new candidate or segment likely to survive hard gates and change the finalist set.

## 9) Apply hard gates in order
Before deep research, remove/deprioritize candidates in this order:
1. wrong product identity/type/variant;
2. hard compatibility/use/fit constraints;
3. strict budget or region/availability constraint;
4. unacceptable safety/channel/maintenance requirement explicitly set by the user or active authority;
5. objectively dominated candidates when another candidate is at least as suitable on all material primary criteria and meaningfully better on one, with comparable availability/risk.

Only then rank remaining products on preferences and value.

## 10) Lock product identity
Before variant-sensitive evidence, establish exact model/variant/generation/size/region/condition as needed.

If identity cannot be locked:
- constrain claims to variant-invariant facts;
- state material uncertainty;
- do not mix materially different variants into one evidence profile.

## 11) Narrow to finalists
For broad mature markets, target **3-5 serious finalists** after hard gates.

Use up to **6** only when distinct tradeoff families genuinely survive.

Use fewer when:
- hard constraints narrow the market;
- only a few credible products exist;
- the user explicitly wants fewer;
- the fixed comparison set is smaller.

Do not retain dominated or unsuitable products merely to fill slots.

Prefer finalists that collectively represent the most relevant tradeoff/design families, not five near-identical products from the same segment.

## 12) Full finalist extraction
For each finalist, normalize the evidence needed to answer the research brief. At minimum, where applicable capture:
- exact identity/configuration;
- market/design segment and intended role;
- hard-constraint fit;
- primary differentiator evidence;
- meaningful mechanism/design explanation behind observed behavior;
- weaknesses/failure modes and conditions that trigger them;
- reliability/QC confidence and time horizon;
- usability/maintenance fit;
- ownership-cost/value posture;
- normal/current price posture and channel when in scope;
- best-fit user;
- user/profile that should choose another finalist;
- important evidence gaps.

Use specialized hierarchy authorities to extend this record for the product domain.

## 13) Candidate x criterion evidence coverage
Apply the finalist coverage matrix from `research_sources.md`.

Before ranking:
- every finalist should have Adequate/Strong coverage on each primary differentiator where that differentiator could change the recommendation;
- investigate asymmetric Weak/Unknown coverage rather than treating missing evidence as inferiority;
- if a critical gap cannot be resolved, expose it and avoid over-ranking the affected candidate.

## 14) Evidence comparability
For every primary differentiator used to compare finalists:
- classify the relevant cross-product evidence as Directly comparable, Directionally comparable, or Not directly comparable under `research_sources.md`;
- prefer competent within-source/common-method comparisons when available;
- do not rank products by tiny differences derived from unlike methodologies;
- if evidence is only directional, phrase the conclusion directionally and reduce confidence as needed.

## 15) Causal / mechanism-based synthesis
For primary recommendation differences, reason beyond adjectives.

Where the domain permits, connect:

`Feature / observed characteristic -> mechanism/design choice -> behavior -> tradeoff -> user consequence -> recommendation effect`

Examples of acceptable depth:
- identify which design characteristic likely explains a measured/observed behavior;
- explain what benefit that behavior creates in the user's workflow;
- identify the paired cost/compromise rather than treating the benefit as free;
- distinguish observed evidence from inferred mechanism when the mechanism is not directly established.

Do not force mechanistic explanation when evidence cannot support it. In that case state the observation and uncertainty.

## 16) Resolve disagreements
When credible evidence conflicts:
- verify variant/revision/test-condition equivalence;
- classify comparability;
- compare methods and competence;
- seek additional independent/common-method evidence if the conflict can change the recommendation;
- narrow the claim or lower confidence if unresolved.

Do not manufacture consensus.

## 17) Current pricing and sellers
If the user provides a budget or asks about current price, deal quality, availability, or where to buy:
- apply the price-state model in `pricing.md`;
- verify current pricing;
- normalize offers fairly;
- apply `seller_instructions.md`;
- distinguish normal product value from temporary deal quality.

If pricing is not material, do not turn research into a seller hunt.

## 18) Synthesis defaults
For open-ended market research:
- give **1 primary recommendation** when evidence supports a clear fit;
- normally give **2-3 alternatives** representing genuinely different tradeoffs/user profiles;
- do not force a single winner when the choice is primarily preference-dependent; state the decision boundary instead.

For each included product state:
- why choose it;
- primary drawback/tradeoff;
- who it best fits;
- relevant value/seller caveat;
- material uncertainty if any.

### Nearest-rival decision boundary
For substantial Standard/Deep research, explicitly compare the primary recommendation to its closest credible rival:
- what actually separates them on the user's primary differentiators;
- why the recommended product wins **for this user**;
- what preference/assumption change would make the rival the better choice.

If no single winner exists, use this same structure to describe the boundary between the strongest options.

## 19) Robustness check
Before finalizing, ask internally:
- Which assumption or weakly supported criterion is most capable of flipping the recommendation?
- Would the winner change if that uncertain point moved against it?
- Is the recommendation driven by a broad pattern or one fragile claim?

If the recommendation is fragile, state the decision boundary/uncertainty rather than presenting false certainty.

## 20) Output
Use `product_research_template.md`.

Keep research briefs, extraction records, coverage matrices, comparability classifications, source-count mechanics, and internal classification out of normal output unless requested or audited.

## Pre-output quality gate
Confirm:
- correct research mode;
- research brief defined;
- discovery target met or justified escape clause;
- market segments actually modeled, not merely named;
- discovery candidates received minimum extraction or clear hard-gate rejection;
- challenge pass completed for Standard/Deep open-market research;
- hard gates applied before preference ranking;
- 3-5 primary differentiators identified;
- finalist count follows defaults or has a reason not to;
- full finalist extraction completed enough for the research brief;
- finalist x criterion coverage is adequate or gaps are bounded;
- comparison evidence is not apples-to-oranges;
- primary differences are explained causally/mechanistically where supportable;
- variant identity is sufficiently locked;
- material claims satisfy evidence packets and stop condition;
- nearest-rival boundary is understood;
- recommendation survives the robustness check or fragility is disclosed;
- product and seller judgments are separated;
- current facts are current enough;
- recommendation reflects user priorities, not generic prestige.
