# Category Authority: Watches / Enthusiast Luxury Goods

## Purpose
Own behavior specific to watches and adjacent enthusiast luxury goods when the `Watches / Enthusiast Luxury Goods` Category is active.

Use the Watches / Enthusiast Luxury Goods sections of `criteria.md`, `source_playbooks.md`, and `pricing.md` alongside this file.

# Shared domain behavior

## Separate tool value, craft value, and collector value
Functional performance, construction/finishing, design/craft, provenance, rarity, and collector interest are distinct value dimensions. A product can be an excellent object of craft or collecting without being the strongest functional value, and vice versa.

## Movement/source claims need precision
For watches, establish the actual movement family/caliber and relevant sourcing/finishing/modification rather than relying on vague "in-house" or prestige language. Evaluate serviceability, parts access, known architecture, and realistic service interval/cost where material.

## Finishing is evidence-sensitive
Case, dial, hands, bracelet/strap, clasp, transitions, alignment, tactile execution, and movement finishing can justify real premiums. Prefer close inspection, specialist review, and ownership evidence over brand photography or prestige inference.

## Provenance and acquisition channel materially affect the object
Authorized dealer, independent dealer, pre-owned specialist, vintage dealer, and auction channels can differ in manufacturer warranty, authentication, condition disclosure, service history, return rights, and price. Keep product merit separate from the channel used to acquire it.

## Hype and ownership satisfaction are different signals
Short-term scarcity, social attention, waiting lists, and collector excitement are real signals but do not automatically establish long-term ownership quality or durable collector appeal.

# Product Research contributions

## Merge: Product Research -> Scope and decision model
When material, add:
- functional/tool use vs dress/design/collector intent;
- size/wearability and bracelet/strap fit;
- movement/service preferences;
- accuracy/water-resistance requirements;
- new/used/vintage posture;
- provenance/authentication tolerance;
- importance of finishing, brand history, rarity, or collector appeal.

## Merge: Product Research -> Market segmentation
Map meaningful families by product philosophy rather than prestige alone: practical/tool, design-led, enthusiast mechanical, high-finish/luxury, independent/artisan, vintage/collector, and other real market structures discovered in the category.

## Merge: Product Research -> Finalist extraction
Add where evidence exists:
- movement identity and serviceability;
- accuracy/performance evidence in context;
- case/dial/bracelet/clasp finishing;
- wearability and dimensions;
- water-resistance/use constraints;
- service interval/cost/access;
- provenance/channel implications;
- collector/community reception separated from current hype.

## Merge: Product Research -> Comparison and evidence use
Do not use brand prestige as a proxy for movement quality, finishing, or long-term ownership. When subjective craft/collector value drives the decision, make that boundary explicit instead of translating it into fake functional superiority.

# Pricing Tiers contributions

## Override: Pricing Tiers -> Tier construction -> linear-ladder assumption
Replace a single ascending functional-value ladder when the market clearly contains parallel value tracks.

Construct separate but comparable regions as needed for:
- **functional/tool value** - timekeeping/use robustness/serviceability and practical execution;
- **craft/finishing value** - case/dial/bracelet/movement finishing, design authorship, handwork, low-volume execution;
- **collector/rarity value** - provenance, scarcity, historical relevance, brand/maker significance, condition, and collector interest.

A product may participate in more than one track. Do not force a craft- or collector-driven premium to prove proportional functional improvement. Preserve the generic requirements for coherent regions, normalized prices, and concrete marginal-spend explanations.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Tier validity
Validate expensive regions using the actual source of value: movement/execution, finishing, service/support, special materials, independent craft, provenance, rarity, or collector appeal. Prestige language alone is insufficient.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Marginal-spend analysis
State which part of a meaningful step-up buys functional refinement, finishing/craft, service ecosystem, brand/design authorship, scarcity, provenance, or collector appeal.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Diminishing-returns interpretation
Identify functional-return flattening separately from continued craft/collector escalation. Do not call post-functional spend irrational when those are the user's explicit priorities.

# Vendor Research contributions

## Merge: Vendor Research -> Ecosystem segmentation
When relevant distinguish manufacturers/maisons, independent makers, authorized dealers, independent dealers, pre-owned specialists, auction houses, and authentication/service specialists. Their roles and evidence are not interchangeable.

## Merge: Vendor Research -> Vendor normalization
For brands/makers add where material:
- movement sourcing/control and serviceability;
- finishing/QC consistency;
- service network, parts policy, and turnaround practicality;
- distribution model and continuity.

For dealers/pre-owned specialists add where material:
- authorization status;
- authentication/provenance process;
- condition grading accuracy;
- service-history disclosure;
- warranty/return terms;
- reputation for resolving authenticity or condition disputes.

# Quick Check contributions

## Merge: Quick Check -> Target identity
Verify exact reference, production/revision period, movement/caliber, size/material/bracelet configuration, condition, box/papers/service history where material, and seller/channel type.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Deal / where-to-buy
For independent-dealer, pre-owned, vintage, or auction offers, add authentication/provenance, condition, service history, manufacturer-vs-seller warranty, fees, and return recourse before judging the apparent discount.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Decision questions
Useful narrow questions include whether the reference/movement is correctly identified, whether condition/service history explains the price, and whether the premium is functional, craft-driven, collector-driven, or channel-driven.
