# Category Authority: Audio / Headphones / Speakers

## Purpose
Own behavior specific to headphones, speakers, and adjacent consumer/enthusiast audio when the `Audio / Headphones / Speakers` Category is active.

Use the Audio / Headphones / Speakers sections of `criteria.md`, `source_playbooks.md`, and `pricing.md` alongside this file.

# Shared domain behavior

## Measurements and listening answer different questions
Use measurements for measurable behavior such as frequency response, distortion, output, latency, battery life, and directivity where applicable. Use competent listening evidence for preference-sensitive traits, ergonomics, spatial presentation, and real-use integration.

Do not convert one into the other. A response curve is not a complete preference model; uncontrolled listening impressions are not substitutes for measured claims.

## Fit and environment are part of performance
For headphones, seal, pad geometry, clamp, and placement can materially change measured and perceived behavior. For speakers, room placement, listening distance, boundary interaction, and dispersion can dominate small specification differences.

## Tuning is not a universal ranking
Neutral, warm, bright, bass-emphasized, studio-targeted, and other tuning choices can be valid for different listeners and uses. Separate execution problems from deliberate voicing and state when the decision is preference-dependent.

## Active products are software products too
For wireless/active audio, include firmware stability, app dependence, codec/connection behavior, battery aging, replaceable components, ANC/transparency behavior, and update history where material.

# Product Research contributions

## Merge: Product Research -> Scope and decision model
When material, add:
- listening use and environment;
- source/device chain and required connectivity/codecs;
- preferred or tolerated tuning traits;
- fit/comfort constraints;
- portability/isolation/mic needs;
- room/placement constraints for speakers;
- active/wireless software and battery ownership concerns.

## Merge: Product Research -> Market segmentation
Map meaningful design families such as open vs closed, passive vs active, wired vs wireless, nearfield vs room speaker, portable vs stationary, ANC/convenience vs fidelity-oriented, and distinct tuning philosophies when these better explain tradeoffs than price.

## Merge: Product Research -> Finalist extraction
Add where evidence exists:
- common-method frequency-response/distortion/output evidence;
- tuning character and its uncertainty;
- fit/seal/comfort sensitivity;
- isolation/ANC/mic/connectivity behavior when relevant;
- active firmware/app/battery history;
- speaker placement/directivity/room interaction;
- repairability and replaceable pads/cables/batteries/components.

## Merge: Product Research -> Comparison and evidence use
Prefer common rigs/methods for small measured differences. Treat cross-rig measurements as directional unless normalized comparability is established. Keep preference-sensitive listening conclusions separate from objective defects or limits.

# Pricing Tiers contributions

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Tier validity
A higher audio tier may be justified by measurable performance, build/comfort, acoustics, connectivity/ANC features, serviceability, or execution consistency. A different tuning preference, luxury finish, or brand cachet is not by itself a universal functional tier increase.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Marginal-spend analysis
State whether extra spend primarily buys measurable acoustic improvement, fit/build, active features, serviceability, lower distortion at demanding levels, aesthetic/craft value, or simply a different tuning target.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Diminishing-returns interpretation
Expect preference and system/room fit to become more important as gross technical defects fall away. Do not imply that expensive equipment produces proportional audible improvement for every listener or setup.

# Vendor Research contributions

## Merge: Vendor Research -> Vendor normalization
For audio brands add where material:
- tuning philosophy consistency across lines;
- measurement/execution track record;
- replacement-component availability;
- warranty/repair practicality;
- firmware/app support for active products.

For retailers add where material:
- demo/audition access;
- return policy for subjective fit/tuning;
- open-box constraints for headphones;
- trade-in or service capability when relevant.

# Quick Check contributions

## Merge: Quick Check -> Target identity
Verify exact revision, region, wired/wireless variant, connector/configuration variant when applicable, and current firmware/app state when it materially affects the question.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Decision questions
Useful narrow questions include whether the target's tuning and fit suit the user, whether the source/device chain supports it correctly, and whether a known firmware/battery/comfort issue changes the judgment.
