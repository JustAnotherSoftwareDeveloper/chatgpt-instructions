# Category Authority: Furniture / Home Decor

## Purpose
Own behavior specific to furniture and durable home-decor purchases when the `Furniture / Home Decor` Category is active.

Use the Furniture / Home Decor sections of `criteria.md`, `source_playbooks.md`, and `pricing.md` alongside this file. Inherit Home & Kitchen behavior.

# Shared domain behavior

## Construction quality must be translated into ownership consequences
Material labels alone are insufficient. Evaluate frame/joinery/substrate, veneer/solid/composite use, upholstery/foam/hardware where relevant, finish execution, repairability, and how those choices affect stability, wear, refinishing, weight, and expected service life.

Do not treat solid wood as automatically superior in every application; engineered materials can be appropriate when their actual construction and use are sound.

## Dimensions include human and spatial fit
Nominal width/depth/height are only the start. Consider seat height/depth, table clearance, door/drawer swing, circulation space, stair/elevator/door access, modular configuration, wall/floor constraints, and user ergonomics where relevant.

## Aesthetics are first-class
Color, finish, texture, proportion, design language, and consistency with the room can legitimately determine the best choice. Separate preference from workmanship defects, and use owner photos/credible design evidence to detect production variation where appropriate.

## Delivery is part of the ownership proposition
Large-item freight, packaging, damage rate, room-of-choice/white-glove service, assembly, damage-claim handling, and return logistics can materially change the purchase. Keep delivery/service failures separate from product-construction defects when evidence permits.

# Product Research contributions

## Merge: Product Research -> Scope and decision model
When material, add:
- exact room and access dimensions;
- user ergonomics/load/use pattern;
- required material/finish/style constraints;
- repairability and expected ownership horizon;
- assembly/delivery tolerance;
- sensitivity to finish/color variation.

## Merge: Product Research -> Hard gates and identity
Add checks for:
- room/access/clearance fit;
- required load or ergonomic fit;
- modular/configuration identity;
- material/finish requirements that are truly non-negotiable;
- delivery feasibility where the item cannot realistically reach or be installed in the space.

## Merge: Product Research -> Finalist extraction
Add where evidence exists:
- construction/joinery/substrate/upholstery details;
- comfort/ergonomics under relevant use;
- finish and production-consistency signals;
- repair/refinish/replaceable-component potential;
- assembly complexity;
- freight/damage/claim experience separated from product defects;
- real owner photos and long-horizon wear patterns.

# Pricing Tiers contributions

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Market segmentation
Allow distinct value families such as flat-pack/value production, mass-market assembled furniture, higher-quality production furniture, design-led premium, custom/semi-custom, and artisan/collector work when the market supports them. These may overlap in price.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Tier validity
A higher region should be justified by construction, materials used appropriately, comfort/ergonomics, finish/QC, repairability, customization, delivery/service, design authorship, or craft. Brand name and visual similarity alone do not validate the premium.

## Merge: Pricing Tiers -> Marginal-spend analysis
State whether extra spend primarily buys longer usable life, better ergonomics, stronger construction, repairability, finish consistency, customization, design/craft value, or logistics/service.

# Vendor Research contributions

## Merge: Vendor Research -> Vendor normalization
For furniture brands/retailers add where material:
- construction transparency and material accuracy;
- finish/color consistency;
- spare hardware/component support;
- damage-claim and replacement behavior;
- threshold vs room-of-choice vs white-glove delivery model;
- assembly service;
- return logistics/cost for large items;
- made-to-order lead-time reliability.

# Quick Check contributions

## Merge: Quick Check -> Target identity
Verify exact size/configuration, material/finish, upholstery option, modular component set, and made-to-order vs stocked status when these change the judgment.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Hard gates
Check room/access dimensions, ergonomic/load requirements, and delivery feasibility before treating style or price as decisive.

## Merge: Quick Check -> Deal / where-to-buy
Include freight, assembly, return pickup/restocking, and damage-claim quality when they materially change an apparent deal.
